# Methods (paper-ready draft)

We evaluated a frozen set of 82,620 synthetic delivery tasks across 4,590 daily seed scenarios. Each of 30 seeds represents one 153-day replicate spanning May to September 2026. The primary analysis used the STRICT_EXCLUDE_GT10KM weather-representativeness audit and a 24 h operating window. A relaxed spatial proxy was retained as sensitivity analysis and was not interpreted as evidence of real-world flyability.

The corrected dispatcher used three petrol vehicles in every mode. M1 launches were restricted to an explicit whitelist of 11 project sites. M2 launches remained vehicle-coupled. Every selected mission included outbound flight, delivery, return launch, recovery, finite battery state, weather checks at four mission events, and a mandatory vehicle return to its depot within the duty window. M1 and M2 shared the same directed geometry, reverse-edge, WMP228-reference, cross-border, weather, and energy-screening kernel.

Outcomes were calculated from physical vehicle legs and UAV flights, avoiding per-order duplication of shared travel. The emissions outcome is a mixed-boundary operational proxy based on petrol use and grid electricity, not a life-cycle assessment. Paired comparisons used the 30 seed-level 153-day replicates as the inference units. Service feasibility required at least 95% completion and 90% on-time delivery across all orders.
