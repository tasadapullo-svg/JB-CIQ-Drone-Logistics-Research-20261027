# Results (paper-ready draft)

The corrected full run retained 82,620 unique tasks, 4,590 scenarios, and 30 seed-level replicates. All corrected modes were evaluated with the same orders and dispatch kernel.

Relative to its shared no-UAV control, M1_DJI increased the operational GHG proxy by 0.16% (0.09% to 0.23%), increased partial OPEX by 0.15% (0.10% to 0.20%), and changed mean delivery time by 0.03% (-0.00% to 0.07%) (Table 5).

Relative to its shared no-UAV control, M1_WING reduced the operational GHG proxy by 0.35% (95% seed-cluster bootstrap interval 0.28% to 0.41%), increased partial OPEX by 0.05% (0.01% to 0.09%), and reduced mean delivery time by 0.14% (95% seed-cluster bootstrap interval 0.09% to 0.18%) (Table 5).

Relative to its shared no-UAV control, M2_DJI reduced the operational GHG proxy by 1.74% (95% seed-cluster bootstrap interval 1.64% to 1.85%), reduced partial OPEX by 0.08% (95% seed-cluster bootstrap interval 0.01% to 0.15%), and reduced mean delivery time by 1.24% (95% seed-cluster bootstrap interval 1.17% to 1.33%) (Table 5).

Relative to its shared no-UAV control, M2_WING reduced the operational GHG proxy by 3.50% (95% seed-cluster bootstrap interval 3.39% to 3.62%), reduced partial OPEX by 0.34% (95% seed-cluster bootstrap interval 0.27% to 0.40%), and reduced mean delivery time by 2.18% (95% seed-cluster bootstrap interval 2.10% to 2.26%) (Table 5).

The correction changed UAV allocation relative to historical S11 (Figure 4). Because fixed-site, event-clock, recovery, geometry, and SOC defects were corrected together, this before-after difference is a multi-factor model revision and cannot be assigned to a single defect. The service-constrained cost-emissions classification is reported only for the tested configurations (Figure 7); nondominance does not imply global optimality.
